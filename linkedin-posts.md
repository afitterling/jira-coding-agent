## Post 1 — The metric we've used for a century just broke

For 100 years we've measured knowledge work the same way: **time in × output out.**

Hours billed. Tickets closed. Lines shipped. Story points burned.

Agentic AI quietly breaks all of it.

Here's the uncomfortable part. Two engineers sit down with the same task. One is fluent with agentic tools — she frames the problem, delegates to a fleet of agents, reviews, and ships in an afternoon. The other works the way he always has and takes three days. Same output. Same quality.

Under the old system, the slower one looks "busier." He logged more hours. He'll often be *rewarded* for it.

That's not a rounding error. That's a measurement system pointing in exactly the wrong direction.

The question every leader is about to face:

**When effort and value decouple, what are you actually paying for?**

I don't think it's time anymore. More on that this week. 👇

## Post 2 — Efficiency is no longer evenly distributed

Old assumption: give skilled people the same tools and they converge to roughly the same speed.

Agentic AI shatters that assumption.

The gap between a skilled AI-native engineer and an average one is no longer 20–30%. It's **multiples.** Not because one types faster — because one knows how to:

- decompose a problem so agents can run it in parallel
- write the spec precisely enough that the system builds the right thing
- verify and reject bad output fast instead of trusting it
- orchestrate ten agents the way a staff engineer once orchestrated ten people

Meanwhile, someone doing identical work manually spends the *same time they always did.* They're not slower than before. They're just no longer on the same curve.

So we now have two populations doing "the same job" at wildly different leverage — and our spreadsheets still treat an hour as an hour.

The skill that's becoming scarce isn't writing the work. It's **directing the work.**

How do you measure *that*? That's the real question. (Post 3.)

## Post 3 — So how do we actually measure it?

If hours, tickets, and lines no longer track value, what replaces them?

My honest answer: we stop measuring *effort* and start measuring *leverage and judgment.* A few candidates I'm watching:

**1. Outcome per cycle, not output per hour.**
What changed for the business, per unit of elapsed time — not how much motion happened inside it.

**2. Leverage ratio.**
How much got produced and *verified* per human decision. The new senior skill is making fewer, better calls that steer more machine work.

**3. Rejection quality.**
In an agentic flow, value is increasingly created by what you *refuse to ship.* The engineer who catches the subtly wrong thing the agent generated is worth more than the one who generates more.

**4. Spec precision.**
The clearer your specification, the better the system builds. Ability to write an unambiguous spec becomes a measurable, teachable, high-value skill.

**5. Idle time is now a feature.**
If your best people look "less busy," that may be the system working — they front-loaded the thinking and let the machine run. Punishing visible idleness will quietly punish your highest-leverage people.

We spent a century optimizing for *busy.* Agentic AI rewards *right.* Most orgs haven't rebuilt their incentives for that yet.

## Post 4 — The shift, in one line

A craftsperson used to be measured by what their hands produced.

A skilled worker in the agentic era is measured by what their judgment *directs* — and what it refuses.

We're moving from **doing the work** to **specifying, steering, and verifying the work.**

The org charts, the comp models, the performance reviews, the timesheets — almost none of them are built for that yet.

That's not a tooling problem. It's a measurement problem. And whoever solves it first will have a very unfair advantage in hiring, retaining, and rewarding the people who actually move the needle.
